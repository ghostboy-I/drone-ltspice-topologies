# Prompt for Fable: 54″ × 18″ composite heavy-lift propeller, full design and verification with OpenFOAM

---

## 0. Role and ground rules

You are the lead aero-structural engineer and research software engineer for a heavy-lift multirotor propeller program.
Your job is to design, simulate, verify and document a **medium-diameter, medium-pitch carbon-composite propeller,
baseline 54″ × 18″**, for a heavy-lift **tricopter (3 rotors, 3 propellers total)** with a **maximum takeoff weight of 280 lb (127.0 kg)**, a **maximum drone
weight of 55 lb (24.9 kg) including batteries**, and a **required range of 10 nautical miles (18.52 km)**, with OpenFOAM as the CFD tool.

Non-negotiable working rules:

1. **No fabricated numbers.** Every result in a table, plot or conclusion must trace to a script output, solver log or
   cited source. If a run fails or isn't converged, say so. Never "estimate" a CFD result and present it as computed.
2. **Hand calculation first, then simulation, then compare.** Every simulated quantity gets an independent analytic or
   lower-order estimate (momentum theory, BEMT, beam theory, CLT), with a percent-difference column. A difference
   > 10% needs an explanation before you move on.
3. **Teach the math.** This is also a learning project. For every method, write the governing equations, state the
   assumptions, and explain why the method fits (or where it breaks down).
4. **Treat the requirement text below as claims to check, not physics to trust.** Some numbers in the brief are
   inconsistent with each other or with the 54″ scale (see §3). Keep a **Claims Register** (claim, source, check method,
   verdict: confirmed / contradicted / unverifiable / rescaled) and an **Assumption Register** (each assumption, value,
   why, sensitivity).
5. **Reproducible.** All parameters live in one `params.yaml`. All geometry, meshes, cases and plots are generated by
   scripts. Pin tool versions. A clean checkout plus `make all` (or an equivalent driver script) must reproduce everything.
6. **Checkpoint with me** at the end of each phase in §5: a short summary of results, the open issues, and what you
   plan next. Stop and ask before spending more than ~12 hours of wall-clock compute on one case, or if a requirement
   is infeasible.

## 1. Mission and what "done" means

Design a propeller family centered on **54″ diameter × 18″ pitch (P/D ≈ 0.33)**, carbon-fiber composite, CW and CCW
versions, that:

- Gives a vehicle **thrust-to-weight ≥ 1.5 : 1 at 100 % throttle** under the **worst design atmosphere** (hot day,
  see §2), with a **target of 2.0 : 1** as a higher safety margin. Report both. (A tricopter cannot survive a
  motor failure, so there is no one-motor-out case; say so in the doc and treat prop/motor reliability margins
  accordingly.)
- Balances hover efficiency with forward-flight efficiency at **12–18 m/s cruise** (design point 15 m/s).
- Matches commonly available heavy-lift motors and ESCs (state the voltage class, motor KV range and ESC current
  rating it implies).
- Meets the five key features in §4 (or shows, with numbers, why a feature must be rescaled for this prop size).

Deliverables are listed in §8.

## 3. Known conflicts in the brief: resolve these explicitly

Address each in the design doc with numbers:

1. **Diameter : pitch ratio.** The feature list says aim for D:P between 4.5:1 and 5.5:1 (for example 60″ × 13″,
   which is 4.6:1). The requested 54″ × 18″ is **3.0:1**. Those can't both hold. Run the analysis for at least:
   - 54″ × 18″ (requested baseline, P/D 0.33)
   - 54″ × 11–12″ (D:P ≈ 4.5–5:1 at the same diameter)
   - 60″ × 13″ (the brief's example)

   Compare hover FoM, cruise efficiency at 12/15/18 m/s, max-thrust RPM, torque, tip Mach and total mission energy
   for the 10 nmi (18.52 km) mission. Then recommend which D:P actually fits the mission (hover share vs. cruise share). The brief itself says
   D:P matters most if > 60 % of flight time is hover/climb and matters less for 12–18 m/s cruise; make that tradeoff
   quantitative with a mission-profile model (takeoff, climb, cruise leg, descent, hover at delivery, reserve).

2. **"≤ 0.1 g imbalance at 10,000 RPM."** A 54″ prop at 10,000 RPM has a tip speed of
   π × 1.3716 m × 10000/60 ≈ 718 m/s (≈ Mach 2.1). It will never run there. Its real max RPM is probably
   ~2000–3000. Rescale the balance requirement using **ISO 21940-11 balance quality grades** (G-grade:
   e_per = G / ω, U_per = e_per × m_rotor). Choose a grade (likely G2.5 or G6.3), compute the permissible residual
   unbalance in g·mm at the real max RPM, and state what "0.1 g" and "±0.03 g" mean (at what radius) if converted
   to g·mm. Then compare.

3. **"Vibration above 1.2 g RMS."** Here "g" means acceleration, not mass. Keep the units distinct throughout.
   Estimate the 1P force from residual unbalance (F = U ω²) and the resulting arm/flight-controller acceleration with
   a simple arm-stiffness/mass model (assumptions logged). Treat the GNSS-drift and gimbal-jitter link as an
   unverified claim.

4. **"Pitch shift > 1.1° reduces thrust up to 9 % in desert conditions."** At 45 °C, sea-level air density is
   ≈ 1.110 kg/m³ vs. 1.225 at ISA, a ≈ 9.4 % drop, so density alone explains roughly 9 % thrust loss at fixed RPM.
   Separate the two effects: (a) thrust change from density at fixed geometry and RPM; (b) thrust change from a
   thermally induced pitch change at fixed density. Report each.

5. **"0.3 mm misalignment raises bearing wear ~40 % over 200 flight hours."** You can't verify that with these tools.
   Record it as unverifiable, but adopt a hub/keyway alignment tolerance (target ≤ 0.1 mm, max 0.3 mm), and compute
   the static unbalance and 1P moment that a 0.3 mm offset produces.

6. **Weight budget vs. range feasibility (do this in Phase 1, before anything else).** A 55 lb drone lifting a
   280 lb MTOW is a payload fraction of ≈ 80 %, far above typical multirotors. Rough check: ideal hover power per
   rotor is ≈ 415^1.5 / √(2·1.225·1.478) ≈ 4.4 kW, so ≈ 6.3 kW per rotor at FoM 0.7 and ≈ 19 kW total; 18.52 km
   at 15 m/s is ≈ 21 min of cruise, so even with cruise power below hover the mission likely needs ~5–7 kWh with
   reserve, i.e., ~20–35 kg of cells at ~200–250 Wh/kg pack level. That alone is close to or above the whole 55 lb
   (24.9 kg) budget, before three ~6–12 kW motors, ESCs, props and frame. Each motor/ESC must also handle roughly
   twice the hover power at T/W 2.0. Build a **mass budget** (props from Phase 5, motors/ESCs from Phase 1
   matching with cited catalog masses, battery from the mission energy, frame and avionics estimated) and state
   plainly whether 55 lb is achievable. If not, give the minimum feasible drone weight, and the payload/range
   trade (payload vs. range curve at fixed 280 lb MTOW). Note that props are where this program can save mass, so
   report prop mass per blade as a design output.

7. **Flex requirement feasibility.** ≤ 1.2 mm tip deflection under 50 N at the tip is a stiff target for a ~0.6 m
   cantilever. Hand calc: δ = F L³ / (3 E I) ⇒ required EI ≈ 3000 N·m² for L = 0.6 m. Check what root thickness and
   layup that implies, the effect of taper, and the weight cost. If it can't be met at a reasonable mass, show the
   tradeoff curve and propose a revised spec.

## 4. The five key features: specification and verification method

Treat each as: requirement → how it's computed → acceptance criterion → result.

### 4.1 Diameter and pitch ratio
- Covered by §3.1 and the BEMT/CFD trade study.
- Acceptance: the recommended geometry meets T/W ≥ 1.5 hot day, and has the best mission energy among the candidates
  that meet the tip-Mach and structural limits.

### 4.2 Static balance tolerance
- Compute the blade-mass, CG-radius and CG-chordwise tolerances that keep the assembled prop within the chosen ISO
  G-grade (from §3.2). Run a **Monte Carlo tolerance study** (≥ 10,000 samples) over blade mass, CG position, hub
  bore runout and bolt-hole position. Report the fraction within spec, and the tolerance that gives ≥ 99 % yield.
- Also check aerodynamic imbalance: thrust difference between the two blades due to ±0.2° pitch mismatch (BEMT),
  which produces a 1P moment.

### 4.3 Material flex index
- Static bench test model: 50 N at the tip, blade clamped at the hub, no rotation. Target ≤ 1.2 mm.
- Analytic: tapered Euler-Bernoulli beam with section EI(r) from classical laminate theory (CLT).
- FE: CalculiX (or another open-source FE solver) shell or solid model with the real layup. Compare with the beam result.
- Also report **in-operation** deflection and tip twist at max thrust with centrifugal stiffening (use pressure loads
  from CFD, mapped onto the structure).
- Compare against glass/epoxy and aluminum versions of the same geometry to confirm or refute the "> 2.5 mm" claim.

### 4.4 Hub interface standard
- Design CW and CCW hubs for M6 and M8 bolt patterns. Specify BCD, bolt count, keyway geometry, and an indexing
  feature that prevents mounting a CW blade on a CCW motor.
- Loads: **centrifugal force** at max RPM (F_c = ∫ m′(r) ω² r dr; for ~0.5 kg/blade at ~3000 RPM this is on the
  order of 10 kN per blade, so check it carefully), thrust, torque, flap and lead-lag bending moments, and 1P
  unbalance loads.
- Checks: bolt preload and clamp loss, bolt shear/tension (VDI 2230 approach, simplified, explained), bearing
  stress at holes in the composite, root shear-out and pull-through. Safety factor ≥ 2.0 on ultimate static loads
  (assumption: confirm), plus a fatigue check (below).

### 4.5 Thermal behavior (TCE)
- Use CLT with temperature-dependent ply properties and ply CTEs from a cited datasheet. Model cure temperature to
  operating temperature (residual stress), and −20 °C to +45 °C.
- Compute the change in twist (pitch) at 75 % radius from thermal and moisture effects for:
  (a) a balanced, symmetric layup; (b) a deliberately unbalanced layup; (c) a "cheap composite" case
  (glass/epoxy or random-mat).
- Acceptance: pitch change within ±0.4° over the range. Feed the pitch shift into BEMT to get ΔThrust, separately
  from the density effect (§3.4).

## 5. Phased work plan

### Phase 0: Environment and reproducibility
- Detect the installed tools and write `ENVIRONMENT.md`: OpenFOAM fork and version (ESI `openfoam.com` vYYMM vs.
  Foundation `openfoam.org` N; **their syntax differs**, e.g., MRF/fvModels setup and the `foamRun` solver interface in
  newer Foundation releases; never mix them), `snappyHexMesh` / `cfMesh`, ParaView/pvpython, CalculiX, XFOIL or
  NeuralFoil, Python packages, CPU core count and RAM.
- **Known machine (checked 2026-11-16):** Ubuntu 26.04, 20 cores, 14 GB RAM. **OpenFOAM 13 (Foundation,
  openfoam.org)** in `/opt/openfoam13` (source `/opt/openfoam13/etc/bashrc`; use `foamRun -solver <module>`
  syntax, e.g., `incompressibleFluid`, `fluid`, and `fvModels`/`MRFProperties` as OpenFOAM 13 defines them).
  CalculiX 2.21 and ParaView 6.0.1 (with python3-paraview) are installed. SciPy 1.16 (apt `python3-scipy`) is installed; Python 3.14 is system-managed, so add other
  packages with apt or a venv, not a global `pip install`.
  14 GB of RAM limits meshes to roughly 5–8 million cells; size the full-rotor AMI meshes to fit, and say so if
  the GCI study needs more.
- If something is missing, say exactly what and how to install it; don't silently substitute.
- Set up `pytest` unit tests for the analytic modules (momentum theory, atmosphere, BEMT with known limits, beam
  deflection against closed form, CLT against a textbook laminate example).

### Phase 1: Sizing and hand calculations
- Atmosphere model for the four design atmospheres (ρ, μ, a).
- Momentum theory: hover induced velocity, ideal power, power at an assumed FoM; forward-flight induced velocity with
  Glauert's formula.
- Thrust and torque coefficients: T = C_T ρ n² D⁴, Q = C_Q ρ n² D⁵, P = C_P ρ n³ D⁵ (state the convention; it
  differs between sources). Required RPM for hover, T/W 1.5 and T/W 2.0, for each atmosphere.
- Tip Mach and tip Reynolds at each point. Chord Reynolds number along the span (expect order 10⁵–10⁶).
- Motor/ESC matching: shaft power and torque at max thrust, required KV at each pack voltage (with a voltage-sag
  margin), phase current, ESC rating. Name the voltage class that gives reasonable currents.

### Phase 2: Aerodynamic design (BEMT)
- Airfoil selection: compute 2D polars at the actual local Re and Mach along the span (XFOIL with compressibility
  correction, or NeuralFoil), including post-stall extension (Viterna) for BEMT robustness. Consider thicker root
  sections for structure and thinner tip sections for Mach. Justify the choices.
- BEMT solver with Prandtl tip and hub loss, handling of axial and edgewise inflow (for forward flight, at minimum an
  azimuthally averaged model with a cross-flow correction; state its limits), and convergence checks.
- Design method: minimum-induced-loss chord/twist (Betz / Larrabee / Adkins-Liebeck) at a chosen design point, then
  a **two-point optimization** (hover at MTOW + cruise at 15 m/s), weighted by the mission profile. Constraints: T/W at
  hot day, tip Mach, minimum root thickness from §4.3, manufacturable chord taper.
- Produce the candidate set from §3.1 and a Pareto plot of hover FoM vs. cruise efficiency vs. mass.
- Output radial distributions of chord, twist, t/c, airfoil, and the CT, CQ, CP, FoM and η curves vs. RPM and
  advance ratio.

### Phase 3: Geometry
- Parametric blade generator in Python (CadQuery, or your own lofting to STEP/STL) from the BEMT output.
  Smooth spanwise blending of sections, a defined tip shape and a root transition into the hub.
- Mirror for CW/CCW, and **verify the mirror is right-handed** (the CCW prop must produce thrust in the same
  direction when spun the opposite way; check this with a quick BEMT or CFD sign test).
- Watertight STL checks (`surfaceCheck`), feature edges for snapping, and a CAD export (STEP) for manufacturing.

### Phase 4: OpenFOAM CFD
**4.0 Validation before design.** Reproduce the **Caradonna & Tung (1981) hover rotor** (NASA TM-81232; two-blade,
untwisted NACA 0012) at a documented case (e.g., 8° collective, tip Mach ≈ 0.44). Get the experimental Cp data
from the report itself and cite it; don't type values from memory. Compare sectional Cp at the measured stations and
the thrust coefficient. Use the same meshing, turbulence model and numerics you'll use for the design. If you can't
get within reasonable agreement, stop and report before going further.

**4.1 Solver choices (justify each):**
- Hover, first pass: steady **MRF** (multiple reference frame) with `simpleFoam` (incompressible) on a
  **1/N_blade periodic sector** using cyclic or cyclicAMI patches. Fast, good for design iterations.
- Compressibility: tip Mach ≈ 0.5–0.6 means density changes of several percent at the tip. Run at least the final
  design with a compressible solver (`rhoSimpleFoam` / `rhoPimpleFoam`, or the fork's equivalent) and quantify the
  difference from incompressible.
- Hover, confirmation: full 360° rotor, transient, sliding mesh (AMI) with `pimpleFoam` (or compressible) for at
  least the final design. Run until thrust is periodic; report the number of revolutions and the rev-to-rev change.
- Forward flight 12 / 15 / 18 m/s: full rotor, transient AMI, freestream at the trim shaft-tilt angle (compute the
  trim angle from a simple vehicle drag model, logged as an assumption). Report mean thrust, torque, in-plane
  H-force, rolling/pitching moments, and the 1P/2P harmonics of blade loads (input to fatigue in Phase 5).
- Turbulence: k-ω SST as the baseline. Discuss whether laminar-turbulent transition matters at these Re (consider
  γ-Reθ SST as a sensitivity run if available).

**4.2 Domain and mesh:**
- Hover domain large enough to avoid recirculation contaminating the inflow: radius ≥ 5D, ≥ 5D upstream and
  ≥ 10D downstream (justify and test; hover CFD in a closed box is known to be sensitive to this). Use appropriate
  inlet/outlet conditions (e.g., total-pressure or `pressureInletOutletVelocity` style) and say why.
- `blockMesh` background + `snappyHexMesh` with prism layers (or cfMesh). Choose a y⁺ strategy: either resolved
  (y⁺ ≈ 1 with low-Re SST) or wall functions (30 < y⁺ < 300); check the achieved y⁺ distribution, don't just
  assume it. Refine the tip vortex region and the wake for at least 1–2 revolutions of the helix.
- `checkMesh` must pass: report max non-orthogonality, skewness, and layer coverage percentage.

**4.3 Verification:**
- **Mesh independence:** three systematically refined meshes (refinement ratio ≥ 1.3), Richardson extrapolation and
  **Grid Convergence Index** (Celik et al. 2008 procedure). Report observed order p and GCI for thrust and torque.
- **Iterative convergence:** residuals down ≥ 4 orders, and force coefficients changing < 0.1 % over the last
  500 iterations (steady) or < 0.5 % rev-to-rev (transient).
- **Time step:** CFL and azimuthal step (≤ 1°/step for the AMI runs, justify).
- Forces from the `forces` / `forceCoeffs` function objects; also integrate sectional loads in post-processing to
  compare spanwise thrust distribution with BEMT.

**4.4 CFD vs. BEMT:** table of CT, CQ, FoM, η for each case, with percent difference. Explain the differences (tip
loss, 3D root flow, wake contraction, compressibility).

**4.5 Compute budget:** state core-hours per case before running. Use `decomposePar` and parallel runs. Prioritize:
validation → final hover sector → final hover full rotor → 15 m/s → 12 and 18 m/s → other candidates.

### Phase 5: Structures and dynamics
- Loads: centrifugal (dominant), aerodynamic (from CFD pressure, mapped onto the structure), gyroscopic during yaw/pitch
  maneuvers and from the tail-rotor tilt servo slewing (estimate rates; the tail prop sees the worst case), 1P
  unbalance.
- Layup design with CLT; section properties along the span.
- Static: stress, strain, failure index (Tsai-Wu or Hashin), safety factor ≥ 2 on ultimate (confirm).
- Bench flex test (§4.3).
- **Modal / Campbell diagram:** flap, lead-lag and torsion modes vs. RPM, including centrifugal stiffening. Keep
  ≥ 15 % separation from 1P, 2P, N_blade·P crossings within the operating RPM range (assumption: confirm). Check
  the motor/arm structural modes if data are available.
- **Aeroelastic checks:** torsional divergence speed and classical flap-torsion flutter margin (a simplified
  2-DOF typical-section estimate at 75 % R is acceptable, with its limits explained). Elastic twist at max thrust
  changes the pitch: feed it back into BEMT and iterate once.
- **Fatigue:** load spectrum from the mission profile (hover cycles, 1P forward-flight loads from CFD), composite S-N
  data with a cited source, Goodman-type mean-stress correction, and a life estimate in flight hours.

### Phase 6: Thermal (§4.5) and atmospheric sensitivity
- Pitch change vs. temperature for each layup, then ΔT, ΔQ and ΔP from BEMT.
- Thrust at fixed RPM across the four design atmospheres. Re-check T/W at hot-and-high; if it fails, report the RPM
  and power needed to recover it, and whether the motor/ESC can deliver it.

### Phase 7: Balance, tolerance, vibration (§4.2, §3.2, §3.3, §3.5)
- ISO G-grade conversion, Monte Carlo tolerance yield, aerodynamic imbalance from pitch mismatch, vibration estimate.

### Phase 8: Integration with the motor drive
- Export the prop load model for the electrical side: Q(ω) curves for each atmosphere and inflow speed, rotor
  polar moment of inertia (blades + hub), and a simple first-order fit Q = k_Q ω² that can drive an LTspice
  behavioral load (a B-source torque in the mechanical-analog circuit). Include the mechanical time constant estimate
  τ = J·ω / (2·Q) around the hover operating point (from linearizing Q = k_Q ω²) so motor-control sims run long enough
  to settle.

1. What was done (two or three lines).
2. Key numbers, with where they came from (file/log).
3. Hand calc vs. sim agreement, and any discrepancy > 10 % with its explanation.
4. Claims/assumptions added or changed.
5. Problems, failed runs, and anything you're unsure of, stated plainly.

Start with Phase 0 and Phase 1, plus your written resolution plan for each item in §3. Don't start CFD until I've
