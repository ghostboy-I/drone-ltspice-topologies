## 1. Verdict in four lines

- **The 55 lb drone weight is not achievable.** The non-battery hardware alone (motors, ESCs, props, frame, avionics) comes to
  34.3 kg against a 24.9 kg limit, before any battery. With the battery for the 10 nmi mission the drone is
  65–84 kg (144–184 lb), and the payload at 280 lb MTOW falls from 225 lb to 136–96 lb.
- **T/W 1.5 on the hot day is marginal and T/W 2.0 is out of reach with the catalog motors found so far.** The best match (U15XL) gives
  T/W 1.46 at 84 V loaded and 1.76 on a fresh pack. T/W 2.0 needs 18.9 kW shaft and 248 A per motor at 24S,
  above the largest motor's 210 A peak rating.
- **Continuous hover is about 2.1× the catalog "rated power" of the biggest single motors.** Hover needs 6.67 kW shaft per rotor
  on the hot day. Three 54″ disks is a high disk loading (281 N/m²). This is a motor thermal risk, not just a mass problem.
- **The mission is cruise-dominated** (83 % of energy in cruise, 14 % of time in hover/climb), so by the brief's own rule the D:P
  ratio is a secondary lever. Disk area and figure of merit are the primary ones.

These are requirement-level infeasibilities (rule 6), so I stop here for your decision before Phase 2.

A useful identity: at a fixed tip Mach number, thrust is `T = CT_h ρ A (M a)² = CT_h A γ p M²`. Temperature cancels. So the thrust
available at the tip-Mach limit depends only on pressure: the hot day costs RPM and power, not Mach-limited thrust, and altitude is what
takes thrust away (T/W at the Mach limit is 1.77 at 1000 m against 2.00 at sea level).

## 5. Momentum theory and rotor sizing

**Hover.** The rotor is an actuator disk that adds momentum to a stream tube. Far below the disk the induced velocity doubles, so
`T = ṁ · 2vi = 2 ρ A vi²`, giving `vi = sqrt(T / 2ρA)` and ideal power `P = T vi = T^1.5 / sqrt(2ρA)`. It assumes uniform inflow,
no swirl, no profile drag and no tip loss, so it is a lower bound. Real power is `P_ideal / FoM`.

**Coefficients.** Propeller convention: `T = CT ρ n² D⁴`, `Q = CQ ρ n² D⁵`, `P = CP ρ n³ D⁵` with n in rev/s, so `CP = 2π CQ`.
Helicopter convention: `T = CT_h ρ A (ΩR)²`. They are related by `CT = (π³/4) CT_h` and `CP = (π⁴/4) CP_h`. Both are reported.

**Sizing logic.** Before BEMT exists, CT is not known, so one design choice closes the problem: *the T/W 2.0 point sits exactly at
the tip-Mach limit (0.60) on the hot day.* That fixes the maximum RPM, and with it the CT the blade must deliver. A fixed-pitch
prop in static conditions has roughly constant CT and CP, so `T ∝ ρ n²`, `Q ∝ ρ n²`, `P ∝ ρ n³` give every other operating point.

## 6. Forward flight (Glauert) and the mission

In forward flight the disk sees its own induced velocity plus the freestream: `T = 2ρA vi sqrt((V cos α)² + (V sin α + vi)²)`,
solved numerically for vi. Trim is a point-mass balance: drag `D = ½ρV² CdA`, tilt `α = atan(D/W)`. Shaft power per rotor is
`P = κ T vi + T V sin α + P0 (1 + 4.65 μ²)` (induced + parasite + profile), with P0 chosen so the same model returns FoM 0.70 in hover.
Limits: no rotor-to-rotor interference, no rotor H-force, hover RPM held in cruise, CdA = 0.60 m² assumed.

## 9. Yaw trim by tail tilt (A3)

Layout assumption: equilateral, hub spacing 1.10 D, so each rotor is 0.871 m from the centre. Tilting the tail rotor by θ gives a side force
`T sin θ` at that arm: `sin θ = N_net · Q / (T · r)`. For a fixed-pitch prop Q/T is constant (72.7 mm), so the angle is the same at
hover and at max thrust.

| Layout | Net torque in hover [N·m] | Tail tilt [°] | Tail lift loss | Side force [N] | Roll to cancel it [°] | T/W 1.5 after tilt | T/W 2.0 after tilt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2+1 (two one way, one the other) | 30.2 | 4.8 | 0.35 % | 35 | 1.6 | 1.498 | 1.998 |
| 3 same direction | 90.5 | 14.5 | 3.18 % | 107 | 4.9 | 1.484 | 1.979 |

Recommendation: 2+1 rotation. It needs 4.8° of tilt and costs 0.35 % of tail lift, against 14.5° and 3.2 % for all-same.
Either way the tilt leaves a steady side force that the vehicle holds with a small roll angle. Strictly, a prop sized for exactly T/W 1.5
ends at 1.498 after the tilt, so A3 needs a fraction of a percent of margin. A tricopter has no motor-out case; a single motor, ESC or
prop failure loses the vehicle, which is why the reliability margins on the prop matter.

## 11. Resolution plan for the §3 conflicts

**3.1 Diameter : pitch.** First-order numbers:

| Candidate | D:P | Geometric pitch at 75 % R [°] | Disk loading [N/m²] | Ideal hover P, hot [kW] | RPM at M 0.60 | T/W at M 0.60 (same CT_h) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 54x18 | 3.0 | 8.1 | 281 | 4.67 | 2987 | 2.00 |
| 54x12 | 4.5 | 5.4 | 281 | 4.67 | 2987 | 2.00 |
| 60x13 | 4.6 | 5.3 | 228 | 4.20 | 2689 | 2.47 |

What Phase 1 already shows: (a) a multirotor cruises with the disk nearly edgewise (3.4° tilt at 15 m/s, so only 0.9 m/s flows axially
through the disk). Pitch does not act as an advance-ratio match the way it does on an airplane; it sets CT, hence RPM, hence the motor KV match
and the section angle of attack. (b) Diameter is the strong lever: 60″ cuts ideal hover power by 10 % and raises the Mach-limited
T/W to 2.47. (c) The blade-element estimate wants 12.4° from the zero-lift line at 75 % R; an 18″ pitch gives 8.1° geometric and a
12″ pitch 5.4°, so with a few degrees of camber the 54×18 is near the needed loading and the 54×12 would have to spin faster,
into the Mach limit. Plan: Phase 2 BEMT on all three (plus the mission model here) for FoM, cruise power at 12/15/18 m/s, max-thrust RPM,
torque, tip Mach and mission energy; then recommend.

**3.2 Balance at "10 000 RPM".** Contradicted: 718 m/s tip speed is Mach 2.11. Real max RPM is 2987. ISO 21940-11,
`e_per = G/ω`, `U_per = e_per · m`, rotor mass 1.24 kg (prop and hub only):

| Spec | e_per [µm] | U_per [g·mm] | Equivalent mass at the tip [g] | 1P force at max RPM [N] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ISO G2.5 | 8.0 | 9.9 | 0.014 | 0.97 |
| ISO G6.3 | 20.1 | 25.0 | 0.036 | 2.44 |
| Brief: 0.1 g, if taken at the tip |  | 68.6 | 0.100 | 6.71 |
| Brief: ±0.03 g blade match, at the tip |  | 20.6 | 0.030 |  |

So "0.1 g at the tip" is 2.7× looser than G6.3, while "±0.03 g" is close to G6.3. Plan: adopt G6.3 as the baseline and G2.5 as the
stretch, then run the Monte Carlo (Phase 7) to find the blade-mass and CG tolerances that give 99 % yield.

**3.3 Vibration "1.2 g RMS".** Acceleration, not mass. The forces above are the inputs; Phase 7 turns them into arm acceleration with a
logged stiffness/mass model. The GNSS-drift and gimbal-jitter link stays unverifiable.

**3.4 "1.1° pitch shift costs 9 % thrust in desert conditions."** Separated: density alone (45 °C against ISA) changes thrust by -9.4 % at fixed
RPM. A ±1.1° pitch change alone changes it by +12.1 % / -11.8 % (blade-element estimate). A 9 % loss from pitch alone needs only 0.83°.
So the brief's 9 % is fully explained by density; a real 1.1° pitch loss would be a second effect of similar size on top. Phase 6 redoes
this with BEMT and the CLT thermal twist.

**3.5 "0.3 mm misalignment, 40 % bearing wear."** Wear claim unverifiable. The unbalance is computable: a 0.3 mm offset of a 1.24 kg rotor is
372 g·mm, 15× the G6.3 allowance, and 36 N of 1P force at max RPM (18 N in hover). Even the 0.1 mm target is 5× G6.3.
G6.3 allows only 20 µm of mass-centre offset, so the hub must either centre to that level or the prop must be balanced on its
hub as an assembly. Plan: keep ≤ 0.1 mm as a machining tolerance and specify assembly balancing.

**3.6 Mass budget.** Done above: infeasible at 55 lb.

**3.7 Flex.** `δ = F L³ / 3EI` gives EI = 3000 N·m² at L = 0.6 m, and 3570 N·m² for the actual free length (0.636 m from a 50 mm hub).
As a *uniform* solid UD-carbon slab the full mean chord wide that is 14.1 mm thick (t/c 12 %) along the entire span; glass needs 20.8 mm and
aluminum 17.4 mm. A real blade tapers to a thin tip, so the target looks very stiff. For the same geometry, deflection scales with 1/E: glass
3.2× and aluminum 1.9× the carbon value (moduli here are placeholders until Phase 5 cites datasheets). Plan: tapered beam with CLT section EI(r),
CalculiX check, a mass-against-deflection curve, and a revised spec if needed.

## 12. Problems and open questions

1. **Decision needed on the weight requirement.** Options: (a) keep 280 lb MTOW and accept a drone of about 144–184 lb with 136–96 lb payload;
   (b) keep the 225 lb payload and let MTOW grow (needs re-sizing, and more disk area); (c) shorten the range; (d) change the configuration
   (larger props, or more rotors) to cut disk loading.
2. **T/W 2.0 has no catalog motor match** at 54″ and 24S. T/W 1.5 is marginal on a sagged pack with the U15XL.
3. Frame mass and CdA are assumptions with no source yet. The frame fraction matters most.
4. The mission model ignores rotor interference and assumes one-way flight with no return leg. Is the vehicle expended, recharged at the
   destination, or does it fly back empty?
