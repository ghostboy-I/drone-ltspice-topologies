## 1. Summary

- **Hover figure of merit from BEMT is 0.84**, against the 0.70 assumed in Phase 1. Hover needs 5.59 kW shaft per rotor on the hot day
  (Phase 1: 6.67 kW). The vendor bench data implied 0.82, so two independent sources now agree and 0.70 was pessimistic.
  CFD still has to confirm it.
- **Mission pack energy drops to 7.47 kWh** (Phase 1: 9.44). Drone weight becomes 130–158 lb and payload
  122–150 lb at 280 lb MTOW.
- **D:P ratio (§3.1): at 54″ the pitch changes mission energy by only 2.8 %, but it decides whether the thrust requirement can be met.**
  The 54×12 (D:P 4.5) reaches only T/W 1.49 at the tip-Mach limit and fails the 1.5 requirement. The brief's 4.5–5.5:1 guidance does
  not fit this vehicle. The design lands at an equivalent pitch of about 23″ (D:P 2.3), on the high-pitch side of the 54×18.
- **T/W with the U15XL motor: 1.79 at 84 V and 1.99 on a fresh pack**, so the 1.5 requirement is met and 2.0 is just missed.
  The prop itself can deliver T/W 2.49 at tip Mach 0.60; the motor is the limit.
- **Diameter is the strong lever:** the 60×13 needs 11 % less energy than the final 54″ design.
- **Forward flight loads are large:** each blade's thrust swings by ±123 N once per rev around a 208 N mean at 15 m/s, and each
  rotor puts a 44 N·m rolling moment into its arm. These drive fatigue (Phase 5).

## 3. Airfoils

**Method.** XFOIL (panel method + integral boundary layer, e^N transition with Ncrit = 9, Karman–Tsien compressibility) on a grid of
thickness [9, 12, 15, 18] %, Re ['1.5e+05', '3.0e+05', '6.0e+05', '1.2e+06', '2.4e+06'] and Mach [0.1, 0.3, 0.45, 0.6]: 77 converged polars plus a
camber comparison. Beyond stall the polars continue with the Viterna–Corrigan flat-plate model, so the solver always has data.
BEMT interpolates linearly in t/c, log Re, Mach and α.

**Camber and thickness comparison** at Re 8e+05, Mach 0.3:

| NACA 2412 | 12.5 | 1.37 | -2.1 | 99 | 0.80 | 91 | 0.0060 |
| NACA 4412 | 12.5 | 1.51 | -4.3 | 123 | 1.18 | 134 | 0.0065 |
| NACA 6412 | 9.5 | 1.66 | -6.4 | 130 | 1.39 | 157 | 0.0083 |
| NACA 4409 | 12.0 | 1.42 | -4.3 | 127 | 1.01 | 131 | 0.0055 |
| NACA 6409 | 10.5 | 1.56 | -6.4 | 140 | 1.29 | 164 | 0.0076 |

**Choice: NACA 44xx, 18 % thick at the root tapering to 9 % at the tip.** Why:
- Hover power for a given thrust goes with cd/cl^1.5, so camber pays: 4 % camber gives a power factor of 134 against 91 for 2 %
  and 64 for a symmetric section.
- 6 % camber is better still on paper (157), but it stalls 3° earlier, has a larger nose-down pitching moment (blade torsion,
  Phase 5) and a stronger suction peak (lower critical Mach). 4 % is the compromise; 6 % inboard is an option to revisit.
- Thick root sections give bending stiffness for the flex requirement; thin tip sections delay compressibility effects.
- The 4-digit family is generated analytically, so the geometry is exact and reproducible. Purpose-built propeller sections could do better.

**Limits to keep in mind.**
- XFOIL with free transition on a clean surface is optimistic on drag. Real blades (roughness, bugs, rain, turbulence) lose some of this.
- **Mach 0.60 polars are not trustworthy.** 3 cases did not converge past a few degrees (4409 Re 1.2e+06 M 0.6, 4409 Re 2.4e+06 M 0.6, 4418 Re 1.5e+05 M 0.6) and were filled from Mach 0.45;
  the others near Mach 0.6 are likely supercritical, where Karman–Tsien is invalid. BEMT therefore has no drag rise. Hover and cruise
  (tip Mach 0.39–0.41) are inside the valid range. Max-thrust numbers above Mach 0.5 are optimistic and need compressible CFD.
- See `results/phase2_polars.png`.

## 4. BEMT

Each annulus has one unknown, the inflow angle φ. Blade-element forces (from the polars at α = θ − φ) are set equal to the momentum
change of the annulus, with Prandtl's factor F for the finite number of blades:

    W sin φ = V + vi          W cos φ = Ωr − vt          k = N c / (8π r F)
    vi = k W cn / sin φ       vt = k W ct / sin φ
    R(φ) = V (sin φ cos φ + k ct) − Ωr (sin²φ − k cn) = 0

The last line is solved by bisection at every station; it stays well behaved at V = 0, which the usual propeller form does not.
Forward flight integrates blade elements over radius and azimuth with a momentum (Glauert) inflow times κ = 1.15 and the longitudinal
Drees gradient. That model is rigid-blade and has no wake distortion or rotor-to-rotor interference.

**Verification** (`tests/test_bemt.py`, `tests/test_design.py`): ideal-twist rotor against Leishman's closed form (CT within 3 %, uniform inflow);
no drag and no tip loss gives FoM ≈ 1; blade-element thrust equals momentum thrust to 1e-6; T ∝ ρn² and P ∝ ρn³ exactly for a
Re-independent section; axial-flight efficiency below the Froude limit; forward flight reduces to hover at V = 0; and the MIL design, built by
separate code, is recovered by the analysis (thrust within 2 %, every section at the design cl).

## 5. Minimum-induced-loss design and the design-RPM sweep

**Betz condition.** Induced loss is least when the wake sheets move as a rigid helix with displacement velocity w. For hover:

    tan φ = (w/2) / (Ωr)      N Γ = 2π r F w sin φ cos φ      c = 2Γ / (W cl)      θ = φ + α(cl)

w is iterated to hit the design thrust. This is the V → 0 limit of the Adkins–Liebeck / Larrabee propeller design. Every section runs at
one design cl. The raw blade has zero chord at hub and tip and a very wide inboard chord, so these rules are then applied: chord ≤ 0.22 R,
tip chord ≥ 20 mm, a tapered shank inboard of 0.25 R, twist ≤ 30°, and a re-twist so each section is back at the design cl.

## 6. Two-point refinement (hover + cruise)

Variables: twist offset, twist slope along the span, chord taper. Objective: mission pack energy from the full BEMT mission (hover-type
segments and the 15 m/s cruise, weighted by their real durations). Constraints as penalties: stall margin ≥ 4°, blade volume no larger,
T/W on the motor within 0.02 of the starting design. Nelder–Mead, 165 evaluations, converged.

Result: twist offset -1.88°, twist slope -6.48° per unit r/R, chord taper +0.000. Pack energy 7623 → 7475 Wh
(**1.9 %**). Adopted as the final design. The change unloads the tip and loads the inboard sections, which lowers the mean cl to 0.90
and suits the lower RPM in cruise. The gain is small because the mission is cruise-dominated and cruise power is mostly induced.

**What it shows.**
1. **Pitch barely moves the energy at a fixed diameter.** A fixed-pitch prop simply settles at whatever RPM gives the thrust; sections with
   decent L/D over a range of cl keep the FoM within 0.81–0.84. In cruise the disk is nearly edgewise (5.2° tilt), so pitch is
   not acting as an advance-ratio match.
2. **Pitch decides the RPM, and RPM decides feasibility.** The 54×12 hovers at 2473 RPM and would need tip Mach 0.60 for T/W 1.5: it
   fails requirement A1 at the Mach limit (T/W 1.49) and fails hot-and-high (1.32). On the KV38 motor at 84 V it reaches only T/W 1.07.
   The 54×18 just reaches 1.50 on that motor; the final design reaches 1.79.
3. **The brief's rule is the wrong way round for this vehicle.** It says D:P matters most when over 60 % of the flight is hover/climb. Here the
   hover-type share is 18 % of energy, and D:P matters anyway, through thrust margin and motor match, not through efficiency.
4. **Diameter matters more than pitch.** The 60×13 uses 11 % less energy, but as a constant-pitch 13″ blade it is Mach-limited to T/W 1.80.
   A 60″ prop with more pitch would be the better design if the airframe can take the diameter. That is outside the 54″ brief, so it is
   flagged, not adopted.

**Recommendation for §4.1:** 54″ diameter with the Phase 2 twist, equivalent to about 54×23 (D:P 2.3). Among the 54″ options that meet the tip-Mach
limit and T/W ≥ 1.5 on the available motor with margin, its mission energy is within 0.3 % of the lowest (54x18).

- Cruise needs less power than hover and less RPM. Energy per km keeps falling up to 18 m/s.
- A rigid fixed-pitch prop cannot flap, so the advancing blade lifts far more than the retreating one. The result is a steady rolling moment
  on each arm (it reverses with rotation direction, so the 2+1 layout leaves one rotor's worth for the flight controller to trim with
  differential thrust) and a once-per-rev blade load of about ±59 % of the mean at 15 m/s. This is the main fatigue input for Phase 5.
- The H-forces add 40 N to the 75 N airframe drag at 15 m/s, which is why the trim tilt is larger than the Phase 1 value.
- "Ideal inflow" is the same condition with κ = 1; the difference is the 1.15 induced-power factor, an assumption that CFD must check.

## 12. Problems and things I am unsure of

1. **BEMT may be optimistic.** Clean-surface XFOIL drag, no compressibility drag rise, 2-D sections, and a forward-flight model with a
   prescribed inflow. The hover number has vendor support; the cruise number has none.
2. **Motor heating is unchanged as a risk.** Hover is now 6.2 kW electrical per motor, still about 1.7× the U15XL "rated power".
3. **T/W 2.0 is not met on a fresh pack and not met on a sagged one**, limited by the motor's 180 A peak rating, not by the prop.
4. The two-point optimiser converged; the remaining gain is likely well under 1 %.
5. Blade mass is only an upper bound until the layup exists (Phase 5). The 151 mm maximum chord and the root shank are assumptions.
6. Tip sections run at Re ≈ 1.6e+05, at the low edge of the polar grid, where laminar separation makes XFOIL less reliable.
7. pytest is still not installed (`sudo apt install python3-pytest`).
