## What each phase found

**Phase 0, environment.** OpenFOAM Foundation 13, CalculiX, gmsh and ParaView are installed. pytest and CadQuery are
missing; tests run through `run_tests.py` (48 pass). OpenFOAM cannot run in a path with spaces, so its cases live in
`~/OpenFOAM/elijah-callahan-13/run/prop54x18` and logs are copied back into the project.

**Phase 1, sizing.** The brief's hand numbers all check out. The 55 lb drone weight does not: non-battery hardware
alone is 34.3 kg against a 24.9 kg limit. Balance "at 10,000 RPM" is Mach 2.1 at the tip and was rescaled to ISO G6.3.
The "9 % thrust loss" is explained by hot-day density alone.

**Phase 2, airfoils and blade design** (blade-element level, hot day, per rotor).

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Hover figure of merit | 0.84 (Phase 1 assumed 0.70) |
| Hover RPM / shaft power | 1932 / 5.59 kW |
| T/W 2.0 point | 2693 RPM, tip Mach 0.54, 15.7 kW |
| T/W on the T-Motor U15XL | 1.79 at 84 V, 1.99 on a fresh pack |
| Mission pack energy | 7.5 kWh |
| Drone weight / payload at 280 lb MTOW | 130–158 lb / 122–150 lb |
| Final blade | two blades, NACA 44xx, 151 mm max chord, equivalent to about 54×23 |

Pitch changes mission energy by under 3 % at 54″ but sets the thrust margin: the 54×12 fails T/W 1.5 at the tip-Mach
limit, so the brief's 4.5–5.5:1 D:P guidance does not fit this vehicle.

**Diameter study.** A 60″ prop needs 13 % less battery energy and lifts T/W, but payload gains only 2–6 lb because
heavier blades and a longer frame use up the battery saving.

**Phase 3, geometry.** CCW and CW propellers as STEP and STL, both watertight. Handedness was verified from the
finished surfaces. The hub is a placeholder with no bore, bolts, keyway or fillet.

**Phase 4.0, validation (in progress).** Rotor data and pressure Table 17 were read from NASA TM-81232 itself; five
entries are flagged as uncertain. The blade-element method over-predicts this rotor's thrust by about 31 %. That is a
caution for the Phase 2 numbers, and CFD is the tie-breaker. The mesh has 1.5 million cells and passes `checkMesh`.

## Open risks

- Blade-element results may be optimistic: clean-surface XFOIL drag, no drag rise above Mach 0.5, simplified forward flight.
- Continuous hover is about 1.7× the motor's listed rated power; no thrust-to-weight figure is thermally verified.
- T/W 2.0 is limited by the motor's 180 A peak rating, not by the prop.
- Forward-flight blade loads are large (±123 N per revolution on a 208 N mean at 15 m/s), which drives fatigue.
- Blade and frame masses are assumptions until the Phase 5 structural design.
- The machine has 14 GB of RAM and swap was full during the solve; closing other apps will speed it up.
- The post-processing code has only been partly tested on real solver output.
